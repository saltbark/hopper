Hopper for iPhone: privacy

Updated 9 November 2026

Hopper for iPhone has no accounts, collects nothing and tracks no one. It is a window onto Hopper running on your own Mac, and what passes between the two is sealed so that only they can read it.

What the app is

Hopper is a terminal app for the Mac that hands work to Claude Code. The iPhone app pairs with Hopper on your Mac and shows its lists and conversations, and lets you answer them, start new ones and move them along. The work itself happens on your Mac. Saltbark LLC makes both.

What we collect

Nothing. There is no sign-up, no analytics, no advertising, no crash reporting of our own and no third-party code in the app. We don't know who uses it.

What stays on your phone

  • The pairing: the phone's own key and what it needs to reach your Mac, in the iPhone's Keychain, on this device only: it doesn't move to another phone, even from a backup.
  • The last thing it showed, so it opens at once: the Mac's latest lists, and text you typed but didn't send. Both are files the iPhone keeps encrypted while it's locked, are not backed up, and are deleted when you unpair.
  • A few settings: the name you gave the phone and the projects you used last.

The camera is used only to read the pairing code, and Face ID or your passcode only to unlock the app. Neither picture nor face leaves the phone, and the app never sees your face data: iOS checks it and tells the app only yes or no.

What passes between your phone and your Mac

Your phone and your Mac don't talk directly. They meet at a relay we run on Cloudflare (hopper-relay.saltbark.com), which passes messages between them. Every message is encrypted on one end and decrypted on the other, with keys made when you paired and fresh keys for every connection, so the relay can't read them, and neither can we.

The relay keeps none of the messages. What it can see, while they pass:

  • the internet addresses of your phone and your Mac, and when they connect;
  • how many messages pass and how big they are;
  • a random code for each pairing (the "room") and a one-way hash of its key, which is the one thing it stores, so that only your phone and your Mac can use that room.

We don't log any of that ourselves. Cloudflare handles the traffic under its own privacy policy.

Apple

Apple delivers the app and its updates. If you choose to share analytics with app developers in your iPhone's settings, Apple may send us anonymous crash reports and usage figures; we use them only to fix problems.

Children

The app is a tool for developers and is not meant for children.

Changes

If this changes, the new version goes on this page with a new date. If the app ever starts collecting anything, it will ask you first.
