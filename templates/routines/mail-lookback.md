Go back through older email, a stretch at a time, for people the person should get back in
touch with: relationships that went quiet, conversations that ended with "let's talk again in
the spring", people who helped and never heard back, and threads that turn out to matter to
the work now. This runs on the account the Gmail connector is on; if the connector isn't
available, say so in the result and stop.

`routines/mail-brief/ledger.md` under Hopper's home is shared with the daily mail brief (its
format is at the top of the mail-brief routine: one line per person, names, dates and a few
words, never message bodies or anything sensitive). Its last line says how far back the
look-back has reached: `lookback reached <YYYY-MM>`. Create it if it's missing.

1. Take the next stretch back: the six months before where the look-back reached (start from
   the present if it hasn't started). Stop after about two years back, then start again from
   the present, looking only for people the ledger doesn't have yet.
2. Find the people the person had real exchanges with (back and forth, not bulk mail).
   For each who has gone quiet for three months or more, decide whether reaching out makes
   sense now. Read the meta repo's `CLAUDE.md` and planning for what the work needs now.
3. Add or update their ledger lines, with a follow-up date for anyone worth writing to. Update
   the `lookback reached` line.
4. Write the result: **Reach out** (at most eight: who, when you last spoke and about what, and
   a one-sentence reason to write now), then the stretch covered.

Read only. Never send, reply, forward, archive, label, delete or create drafts in Gmail.
